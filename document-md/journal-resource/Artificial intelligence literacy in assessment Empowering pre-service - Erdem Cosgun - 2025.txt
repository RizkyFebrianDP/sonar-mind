Key insights

What is the main issue that the paper addresses?
This study explores pre-service EFL teachers’ experiences with MagicSchool, an AI 
tool for exam preparation. It examines both its benefits—such as efficiency, practi-
cality and adaptability—and its challenges, including prompt writing difficulties, veri-
fication concerns and cultural mismatches, highlighting the need for AI literacy and 
ethical considerations in teacher education.

What are the main insights that the paper provides?
The study shows that while MagicSchool helps teachers create exam questions 
more easily and customise assessments, its limitations—such as creativity con-
straints and contextual inconsistencies—reduce its overall effectiveness. These 
findings highlight the importance of AI literacy in teacher training to ensure ethical 
and effective use of AI tools in language education.

INTRODUCTION
Artificial intelligence (AI) is becoming a powerful force in education, fostering a new model of 
collaboration between humans and machines that is reshaping educators’ role (Moorhouse 
& Kohnke, 2024). Artificial intelligence tools open new avenues for enhancing educational 
quality and boosting teaching efficiency (Mustafa et al., 2024), supporting educators in aca-
demic tasks like curriculum planning, selecting effective learning activities and designing 
personalised learning practices. Notably, AI is also making a significant impact on exam 
preparation (Rivera-Rosas et  al.,  2024), as generative AI (GenAI) tools are increasingly 
praised for their ability to innovate by automating test item design, practice test generation 
and providing automated feedback (Xi, 2023). This integration of AI not only eases educa-
tors’ workload but also enriches students’ learning experiences by offering more individual-
ised and adaptive options.

The adoption of AI is particularly relevant in English language education, which is consid-
ered to be a key area for AI integration (Crompton & Burke, 2023). Consequently, teacher 
education programmes must prioritise AI literacy, training teachers with the essential digital 
skills to successfully navigate an increasingly automated educational landscape (Adeshola 
& Adepoju,  2023). This preparedness extends beyond basic technical skills to include 
the use of digital tools for optimising educational outcomes. Despite the potential of AI in

address these challenges by helping educators streamline the assessment process and 
enhance learning products (Zhai et al., 2021). However, concerns about the responsible use 
of AI in assessment highlight the need for best practices (Ciampa et al., 2024). To better 
understand how English as a foreign language (EFL) teachers use AI-powered tools, this 
study adopts the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) (Marikyan

proach to examine technological acceptance and includes performance expectancy, effort 
expectancy, social influence and facilitating conditions. First, performance expectancy di-
rectly relates to the first research question, as it examines how pre-service EFL teachers 
perceive MagicSchool's impact on their pedagogical approaches and assessment skills in 
exam preparation. Effort expectancy, on the other hand, considers how easy or challenging 
they find the tool to use, influencing their willingness to integrate it into their teaching prac-
tices. Social influence connects to the second research question by exploring how external 
factor shape teachers’ attitudes towards AI adoption in assessment. Finally, facilitating con-
ditions encompass ethical concerns highlighting factors that mediate pre-service teachers’ 
acceptance of MagicSchool. By applying UTAUT, this study explores the intersection of AI 
literacy, pedagogical decision-making and ethical considerations in pre-service teachers’ 
engagement with MagicSchool.

Thereby, the purpose of the study is to explore pre-service English language teachers’ 
perceptions of MagicSchool, an AI tool specifically designed for educational contexts. Unlike 
more general AI tools, MagicSchool was generated to support educators in academic set-
tings, making it especially suited for language assessment. By examining pre-service teach-
ers’ experiences with this tool, the study explores two key questions:

1.	 How does the use of MagicSchool influence pre-service EFL teachers’ pedagogical 
approaches and assessment skills in exam preparation?

2.	What factors shape pre-service EFL teachers’ adoption of MagicSchool, and how do AI 
literacy and ethical concerns mediate this process?

This investigation offers significant insights into pre-service teachers’ adaptation of AI 
in language assessment and underscores AI literacy within teacher education to prepare 
future educators for the evolving demands of their profession.

LITERATURE REVIEW
AI in education and teacher preparation
AI means how a system interprets received information, learns and applies knowledge to carry 
out specific tasks through adaptable responses (Amin et al., 2024). While AI in education has 
evolved significantly, its impact on teacher preparation remains an emerging area of study, 
with both opportunities and challenges. Its integration into education began in the 1950s with 
computer-assisted instruction, marking the first use of AI in learning (Chan & Tsi, 2023). Over 
time, this evolved into advanced applications, enhancing learning by providing interactive, re-
sponsive environments. However, the extent to which these tools improve teaching efficacy 
and pedagogical strategies remains debated (Zawacki-Richter et al., 2019).

Artificial intelligence technologies like natural language processing and machine learn-
ing hold a great ability to reshape education (Zawacki-Richter et  al.,  2019). Research 
shows AI's advantages in providing adaptive, customised learning experiences for students

educators, such as processing language, following instructions and answering queries, al-

provide individualised feedback and can manage administrative duties like attendance 
tracking, assignment monitoring and paperwork, freeing educators to focus on curriculum 
planning and student support (Chen et al., 2020). Nonetheless, research indicates that while 
novice English language teachers acknowledge AI's potential, pre-service teachers often 
lack the digital literacy and pedagogical knowledge necessary to integrate these tools effec-
tively (Moorhouse, 2024). Closing this gap is crucial as AI becomes a central part of modern 
education, helping educators enhance classroom instruction and streamline administrative 
tasks (Chen et al., 2020; UNESCO, 2021). Thus, the study addresses these gaps by inves-
tigating how pre-service teachers perceive AI in their training, focusing on their AI literacy, 
challenges in adoption and its impact on their teaching preparedness.

AI's role in enhancing language learning
Artificial intelligence has significantly transformed English language teaching (ELT), offer-
ing both opportunities and challenges (Edmett et  al.,  2024). GenAI, capable of creating 
original content, has been particularly impactful in language learning by facilitating person-
alised learning (Chiu, 2024). A review study of AI in ELT from 2014 to 2022 highlighted five 
key areas of application: speaking, writing, reading, pedagogy and self-regulation (Edmett 
et al., 2024). While these studies emphasise AI's benefits, they also highlight concerns re-
garding its limitations and uneven adoption.

For example, voice assistants like Alexa help learners practice speaking, yet they were 
likely to abandon using the tool when faced with difficulties in IPA (International Phonetic 
Alphabet) (Dizon & Tang, 2020). Tools like Grammarly provide automated feedback on writ-
ing, but they may not effectively support higher-order writing skills, such as argumentation 
(Dizon & Gayed, 2021; Nazari et al., 2021). Artificial intelligence games support vocabulary 
acquisition (Zheng et al., 2015), yet some studies question their impact on long-term reten-
tion Similarly, AI chatbots support listening comprehension but lack human-like conversa-
tional adaptability, limiting deeper interaction (Hew et al., 2023).

In addition, AI literacy among pre-service and in-service teachers remains a challenge. A 
study of Slovak EFL pre-service teachers found that although many used AI tools regularly, 
they lacked confidence in applying them in teaching contexts (Pokrivcakova, 2024). Similarly,

AI's influence on assessment practices in education
Artificial intelligence is transforming assessment by offering more efficient, personalised 
and data-driven evaluations. However, while research highlights AI's potential, its full impact 
remains debated, with concerns over reliability, ethical implications and the necessity of 
human oversight (Grassini, 2023). Historically, educational assessments have been manual, 
often constrained by subjectivity, time limitations and a teacher-centred approach (Penny & 
Coe, 2004). GenAI has begun to challenge these limitations, enabling personalised learning 
and more dynamic evaluation methods (Chiu, 2024).

Assessment is crucial for monitoring student progress and adjusting teaching strate-
gies (Wang et al., 2023). Artificial intelligence tools have been particularly effective in lan-
guage proficiency evaluations, improving the accuracy of assessing grammar, vocabulary 
and speaking skills (Lukácsi, 2020). However, concerns remain about over-reliance on AI-
generated feedback, potential biases in automated grading and the loss of human judge-
ment in nuanced assessments (Ciampa et al., 2024). Nevertheless, AI's ability to enhance 
assessment speed and precision suggests that it may outperform traditional methods in 
specific contexts (Stоšić & Malyuga, 2024).

Looking ahead, AI is unlikely to replace human-led assessments entirely but rather com-
plement them through hybrid models that integrate human judgement and AI-generated 
insights (Grassini,  2023). This shift requires educators to develop AI literacy and critical 
evaluation skills to effectively interpret AI-generated results. Technology-enhanced assess-
ments, as noted by Williyan et al. (2024), offer significant opportunities for improving eval-
uation methods. However, broader discussions in literature highlight challenges related to 
transparency, accessibility and equity (Bulut et al., 2024).

One emerging tool in AI assessment is Magic School AI, a platform designed specifically 
for educators and utilised by over 1.5 million educators worldwide (Mustafa et al., 2024). 
Unlike general AI tools like ChatGPT, which require significant prompt engineering and man-
ual adjustments, Magic School AI provides structured prompts, reducing cognitive load and 
streamlining workflows for teachers (Setyaningsih et al., 2024). However, while these ben-
efits are promising, additional research is necessary to assess their ongoing effectiveness 
and potential drawbacks, and whether AI platforms reinforce or mitigate existing assessment 
biases. Thus, this study examines the use of Magic School AI into assessment practices, 
considering both its potential to enhance accuracy and efficiency and the broader concerns 
surrounding AI's role in education.

AI literacy, limitations and ethical considerations
While AI offers transformative potential in language teaching and assessment, its integration 
raises concerns regarding accuracy, bias, ethical responsibility and over-reliance on auto-
mated tools. Artificial intelligence literacy is an important skill for educators, supporting them 
to critically assess AI's capabilities, limitations and ethical implications (Ng & Chu, 2021). 
Artificial intelligence platforms are being used to personalise learning, analyse students’

efficiently but also critically assessing its outputs and ensure responsible use. Developing AI 
literacy involves understanding AI's strengths and weaknesses, questioning its recommen-
dations, and integrating human oversight in decision-making processes (Wang & Lu, 2023). 
Institutions that prioritise AI literacy training will be better positioned to balance innovation 
with ethical responsibility, maintaining high educational standards (Zhou et al., 2024). Ethical 
considerations are also central to AI integration in education. Issues like data privacy and 
academic integrity require careful oversight to prevent potential misuse (Xia et al., 2024). 
Artificial intelligence-assisted grading, for instance, has been criticised for lacking trans-
parency and reinforcing biases embedded in training data (Chan & Tsi, 2023). Thus, this 
study examines how educators can effectively integrate AI into assessment practices while 
addressing its limitations and ethical considerations. A key focus is Magic School AI, a tool 
designed specifically for educators, which offers structured prompts to support assessment 
practices. By investigating AI literacy, this study is anticipated to contribute to the broader 
discourse on responsible AI adoption in education.

METHODOLOGY
Design
A case study that enables the investigation of recent phenomena within their real-life con-
texts, particularly when the borderline between the phenomenon and context is not clearly 
defined (Yin, 2003), was adopted in this study. Through this methodology, the exam prep-
aration experiences of senior pre-service EFL teachers were investigated in depth. This 
approach provides a holistic understanding of how pre-service EFL teachers used and re-
flected on the use of an AI tool, MagicSchool, in exam preparation.

Context of the study
The study took place in the Department of ELT at a state university in Türkiye. The de-
partment offers undergraduate, Master's (MA) and Doctoral (PhD) programmes to train 
educators and researchers in language teaching. At the undergraduate level, fourth-year 
pre-service teachers take a compulsory Exam Preparation in ELT course, which equips 
them with the skills to design and implement effective language proficiency assessments. 
The course combines theory and practice: the first 9 weeks cover foundational principles, in-
cluding exam formats, language skill assessment (reading, writing, listening, speaking, and 
grammar) and test design best practices; the final 5 weeks focus on hands-on application, 
where students develop comprehensive language proficiency exams.

As part of the practical component, students utilised MagicSchool, an AI-based educa-
tional tool, to design exams assessing all four language skills. The tool was chosen for its 
education-specific design, offering features like automated rubric generation, proficiency-
based question differentiation and structured prompts. Unlike general AI tools like Microsoft 
Copilot or Chat GPT, it provides pre-set templates (Setyaningsih et al., 2024) that support 
pedagogically sound exam creation while easing cognitive load. Its curriculum-focused ap-
proach made it particularly relevant to our study's goals. Integrating AI technology enabled 
pre-service teachers to explore innovative assessment design, critically evaluate its peda-
gogical potential and reflect on its impact. By situating the study in this context, the research 
underscores the value of blending traditional pedagogy with emerging technology to equip 
future educators for evolving challenges in language assessment.

Participants
Participants were 27 senior pre-service EFL teachers at a state university in Türkiye. Among 
them, 19 of them were female and eight were male and their ages ranged from 21 to 24. 
They were selected through purposive sampling as suitable to the aim of the study. A demo-
graphic survey given at the beginning of the semester, showed that 97% of the participants 
were already familiar with AI, especially Chat GPT, which they had previously utilised for 
various teaching purposes, including translation, lesson planning and exam preparation. 
This prior exposure suggests that many participants entered the study with a foundational 
understanding of AI tools, potentially shaping their perceptions and expectations regard-
ing AI integration in assessment. However, their familiarity was largely limited to general 
AI applications rather than specialised educational tools. Only 8% of the participants had 
prior knowledge of MagicSchool, indicating that the tool itself was relatively new to most of 
them. This limited familiarity may have influenced their initial engagement with MagicSchool, 
requiring a learning curve before they could fully explore its features and pedagogical ap-
plications. To provide a structured context for the study, all participants were enrolled in a 
course titled Preparing Exam Questions in ELT. Within the scope of this course, they were 
introduced to the AI tool MagicSchool, which was later employed to design exam questions. 
This hands-on integration of MagicSchool offered participants practical exposure to AI-
supported assessment development, enabling them to explore its potential and limitations in 
ELT. The careful selection of participants with diverse backgrounds and prior AI experience 
ensured rich insights into AI integration and teacher education.

Data collection
Data were collected between April and June 2024 in multiple ways (Figure 1). Primary 
tools included focus group interviews and reflective reports. The process began with a 3 
h training session introducing AI in education and MagicSchool's functionalities. This ses-
sion featured an instructor-led presentation, AI tool demonstrations and a hands-on activ-
ity where pre-service teachers used MagicSchool to create sample questions. Following 
the training, participants were given the School Life unit from the 10th-grade curriculum 
and tasked with designing an exam assessing all four language skills using MagicSchool. 
After completing the task, they wrote reflective reports detailing their perceived successes 
and challenges. While self-reported data has limitations, this study prioritised capturing 
participants’ perspectives for meaningful insights. Moreover, semi-structured interviews 
were done with eight randomly selected participants to explore the pedagogical benefits 
and challenges of MagicSchool, as well as their familiarity and ease of use. Combining

Data analysis
Thematic analysis, in which patterns are identified, organised and interpreted, was selected 
to analyse the data (Braun & Clarke, 2006) via MAXQDA. Through this method, key themes 
were uncovered, providing insights into participants’ experiences with integrating the AI tool, 
MagicSchool, in exam preparation. Codes emerged directly from the data, aligning with 
an inductive approach. However, the research questions, in conjunction with insights from 
prior studies, inevitably influenced the way texts were analysed, codes were developed and 
themes were identified.

To ensure credibility, a multi-tiered approach was employed. The primary researcher 
conducted initial coding, while a language education expert independently coded 10% of 
the data, achieving a high inter-coder reliability (0.80). ChatGPT, used as a third coder, 
demonstrated comparable reliability (0.76). Discrepancies were resolved through discus-
sions, with a third expert consulted when necessary. Transferability was supported through 
detailed participant descriptions, allowing readers to assess applicability to similar contexts. 
Trustworthiness was maintained through transparent coding, systematic cross-checking 
and confidentiality measures.

DISCUSSION
This study found that MagicSchool enhanced exam preparation by improving efficiency, 
effectiveness, practicality, personalisation, creativity, and adaptability. However, challenges 
related to prompt writing and verification requirements, potential adverse effects on teach-
ers’ creativity and the quality of outputs, and cultural and contextual mismatches limited its 
pedagogical benefits. The coexistence of these advantages and limitations highlights the 
complexity of integrating AI into assessment practices, underscoring the significance of AI 
literacy and ethical awareness in effectively adopting AI tools for educational assessment.

Participants reported significant improvements in efficiency, effectiveness, and practi-
cality, aligning with Xi's (2023) utterance on AI's role in automating complex tasks and op-
timising assessment design. Among them, time efficiency emerged as the most prominent 
pedagogical advantage, as also noted by Hashem et al. (2023). Participants also mentioned 
that it could generate diverse question types, facilitate distractor creation, and tailor mate-
rials for students at different proficiency levels, proving its adaptability in meeting diverse 
educational needs (El Naggar et  al.,  2024; Grassini,  2023). These findings reinforce the 
broader argument that AI platforms can personalise learning, analyse student proficiency 
and create interactive instructional materials (Berendt et  al.,  2020; Williamson,  2020). 
Additionally, practicality of MagicSchool was another pedagogical advantage mentioned by 
participants. The results showed that participants could easily manage the tool, probably 
owing to MagicSchool AI's education-specific design. While some had used AI tools like

to address contextual limitations. Additionally, as Stоšić and Malyuga  (2024) highlight,

approach where AI supports rather than replaces educators (Xi, 2023). Developing AI 
literacy and critical evaluation skills is therefore essential for teachers to effectively inter-
pret and refine AI-generated exam materials (Grassini, 2023).

Overall, this study highlights opportunities and challenges in MagicSchool integration into 
exam preparation. This study stresses both the benefits and challenges of using MagicSchool 
in exam preparation. While it enhances efficiency, adaptability, and practicality, issues with 
prompt formulation, verification and over-reliance on AI underscore the necessity for careful 
application. These findings stress the importance of AI literacy and human oversight to en-
sure AI complements, rather than replaces, teacher agency in assessment design.

Limitations and suggestions for further studies

deeper understanding of how AI influences pedagogical development, particularly in exam

multiple AI tools to assess their effectiveness in different assessment contexts, offering 
a more comprehensive perspective on AI integration in teacher education. Examining the 
impact of AI literacy training on pre-service teachers’ professional development through 
longitudinal studies would also provide valuable insights into how educators adapt to and 
incorporate AI into their instructional practices.
